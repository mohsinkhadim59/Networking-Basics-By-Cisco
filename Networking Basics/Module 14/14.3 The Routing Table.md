## How a Router Forwards Packets Between Networks

### Host Sending to a Remote Network
- **H1 (10.0.0.1)** wants to send a packet to **H4 (192.168.1.2)**, which is on a **different network**.  
- The packet contains:
  - **Source IP:** 10.0.0.1  
  - **Destination IP:** 192.168.1.2  
- **H1 sends the packet to its default gateway (the router)** because the destination is not on its local network.

![Router Forwarding Example](/Imagesn/117.png)

---

## Router Processing the Packet
- The router receives the frame and examines the **destination IP address**.
- It checks its **routing table**.
- It sees that **192.168.1.2 belongs to the 192.168.1.0 network**, which is reachable through its **FastEthernet0/2** interface.
- The router then **forwards the packet out FastEthernet0/2** toward the final destination.

## What Happens With Broadcast Traffic
- H1 sends a packet to **255.255.255.255** (the IPv4 broadcast address).  
- Broadcasts are meant for **all devices on the same local network**.

![Broadcast Packet](Imagesn/119.png)

- The router **receives** the broadcast but **does NOT forward** it to other networks.
- Broadcasts stay **within the same broadcast domain**.
