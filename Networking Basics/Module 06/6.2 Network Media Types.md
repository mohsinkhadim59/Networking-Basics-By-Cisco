# **Network Media – Summary**

Network media provides the **connectivity and path** that data travels through in a network.  
Modern networks use three primary types of media:

- **Wireless (through the air, no physical cable)**
    
- **Copper cabling**
    
- **Fiber optic cabling**
    

---

## **1. Copper: Twisted Pair (UTP)**

### **Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP)**

- Most common Ethernet cable type.
    
- Contains **8 wires**, arranged in **4 color-coded pairs**.
    ![UTP](/Imagesn/56.jpg)
- Used with **RJ45 connectors**.
    ![RJ45](/Imagesn/57.jpg)
- Connects devices such as **network cards, switches, routers**.
    
- Must follow wiring standards to work properly.
    

### **Key Characteristics**

- Very common in LANs.
    
- Inexpensive and easy to install.
    
- Susceptible to electromagnetic interference (EMI).
    

---

## **2. Copper: Coaxial Cable**

### **Coaxial Properties**

- Contains a **single solid copper core**.
    
- Surrounded by:
    
    - **Plastic insulation**
        
    - **Metal mesh shielding** (protects against EMI)
        
![Copper Coaxial Cable](/Imagesn/54.jpg)
### **Connectors**

- **BNC connector**
    ![BNC Connector](/Imagesn/58.jpg)
- **F-type screw-on connector**
![F Type Screw On Connector](/Imagesn/59.jpg)
    

### **Common Uses**

- Cable TV systems.
    
- Older Ethernet networks (legacy).
    

---

## **3. Fiber Optic Cable**

### **How It Works**

- Made of **glass or plastic**.
    
- Light travels through the core and reflects along an outer layer (cladding).
    
- Carries **light signals**, not electrical signals.
    
![Fiber Optic Cable](/Imagesn/55.jpg)
### **Key Advantages**

- **Immune to electromagnetic interference (EMI)**.
    
- Supports **long-distance transmission** (miles).
    
- Significantly **higher speeds** compared to copper.
    
- More flexible applications due to multiple fiber types and light sources.
    

### **Common Uses**

- Long-distance communication.
    
- High-speed backbone links.
    
- Connections between switches within buildings (short-range fiber).

# **Three Media Types **

Data must travel across a network from a **source** to a **destination**, and this happens through **network media**.  
Network media acts as the **channel** that carries the transmitted data.

Modern networks use **three primary types of media**:
![Media Types](/Imagesn/60.png)
---

## **1. Copper (Metal Wires in Cables)**

- Uses **electrical impulses** to transmit data.
    
- Common types: **UTP, STP, Coaxial**.
    
- Widely used in Ethernet networks.
    

---

## **2. Fiber-Optic (Glass or Plastic Fibers)**

- Uses **pulses of light** to carry data.
    
- Immune to electromagnetic interference.
    
- Supports extremely high speeds and long distances.
    

---

## **3. Wireless (Radio Waves / Electromagnetic Waves)**

- Data is transmitted through the **air**.
    
- Uses **modulated electromagnetic frequencies**.
    
- Common examples: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular networks.
    

---

>[!Notes] **Criteria for Choosing Network Media**
When selecting a type of network media, consider these **four key factors**:
>### **1. Maximum Distance**
>- How far can the media carry a signal without loss? 
>### **2. Installation Environment**
>- Is the area prone to interference, harsh conditions, or obstacles?
>### **3. Data Volume and Speed**
>- How much data needs to be transmitted?
>- What bandwidth or throughput is required?
>### **4. Cost**
>- Cost of the media itself and the labor/installation required.
